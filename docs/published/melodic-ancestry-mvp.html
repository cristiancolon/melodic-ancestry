MVP brief scoping August 2026

Three routes to a first version

The research settled what is buildable and what isn’t. This is the follow-on question: given that, what do we actually build first — and what does each option cost, prove, and foreclose?

The recommendation, up front

Build the Ledger first — but as a three-week probe, not a product. Its only job is to produce one number: how many directed, dated derivation edges actually exist in the curated layer. That number decides everything downstream.

Then build the Rarity Index. It is the thing with a buyer, it never has to answer “who copied whom,” and it is the denominator every later ancestry claim needs anyway.

The Trace — snippet in, lineage out, the demo we started from — is not an MVP. It is what the Rarity Index earns the right to attempt. It cannot even state its own abstention rule until the index exists.

01

What the research found

Seven findings, in the order they depend on each other. Each one narrows what an MVP can honestly be.

  1. Solved

    Finding similar melodies is a solved-enough engineering problem.

    Cover-song identification via fixed-length embeddings works at catalog scale and is open source. ByteCover reports mAP 0.836 on SHS100K, and its embeddings compress to 128–256d with graceful loss — which is what turns retrieval into ordinary vector search instead of exhaustive DTW.

  2. Not solved

    Ancestry is not solved by anyone.

    Across every verified system, directionality — who copied whom — was supplied by human curation, encoded by construction, or decided by manual musicological judgment. It was never learned from the music. Similarity is symmetric by construction: BMM‑Det’s edit costs are symmetric and its matching is invariant under swapping the two works; no chronology enters the pipeline at any point.

  3. Mechanism

    The two systems that do produce ancestry get direction from outside the audio.

    Bryan & Wang (ISMIR 2011) built an influence network entirely from 42,447 hand-curated WhoSampled records, each already a directed source→destination pair with a release year. Genome of Melody (2025) builds real derivation trees, but rooting them failed: both top candidate roots were historically implausible and the authors re-rooted by hand.

  4. Wall

    Synthetic training data does not transfer to real material.

    Every system in this space reports two numbers — one on its own data, one on real material. The spread is the whole story, and a learned model losing to plain DTW is a signal that the training distribution is wrong, not the architecture.

  5. Binding constraint

    Ground truth is roughly a hundred examples.

    Any precision number in this field rests on about a hundred real pairs total. This caps how confidently anything here can be evaluated — including our own work.

  6. The opening

    The defensible product is a prior-art index, not an originator oracle.

    Yen (58 U.C. Davis L. Rev. 553, 2024) draws the line courts actually use: rarity evidence is admissible, similarity verdicts are not. In Johannsongs-Publishing v. Lovland an expert report was excluded under Rule 702 for calling features “highly doubtful” to occur elsewhere without having analyzed prior art. “This figure appears in N recordings, M of them predating X” is evidence courts already credit: in Structured Asset Sales v. Sheeran (aff’d 2d Cir. 2024) the court counted twenty-nine prior uses of the progression and twenty-three more before the defendant’s release, and that enumeration carried the holding. What no court has yet seen is such a count produced computationally rather than hand-compiled — that form is untested. And it is symmetric, so it sidesteps direction entirely.

  7. Boundary

    “Find the original” may be ill-posed for the material hooks come from.

    Savage et al. found zero highly-related cross-tradition pairs; the 2025 phylogenetics rooting failed outright. For folk and public-domain sources, the honest output is “no single originator” — which means abstention is a feature to design in, not an error path to bolt on.

02

Three routes

Same goal, three different bets about which unknown is worth testing first. They are not variations on one plan — each one buys a different piece of information, and each one is genuinely shippable on its own.

The Ledger

Recommended first — as a probe

Ship: a queryable derivation graph with a lineage browser. Type a work, get its ancestors and descendants with dates, ranked by Katz centrality. No audio, no ML, no inference.

Settles
How much ancestry is already written down. Count the directed, dated work→work edges; count chains of length ≥3; check how many of twenty reference hooks appear at all. That coverage number decides whether there is a product in the undocumented tail or not.
Build
MusicBrainz bulk dumps into Postgres — the API caps at 1 request/second, so crawling is not an option. Wikidata dumps or SPARQL for P144/P4969. Join release dates, materialize an edge table, run Katz over it with an α sweep, put a thin UI on top.
Estimate
2–3 weeks · one engineer · no GPU · no licensing
What kills it
Nothing kills it — but sparse coverage demotes it from product to instrument. Wikidata’s own constraints discourage P144 on audio tracks, so expect the recording-level graph to be thinner than the work-level one.
Can’t do
Anything not already curated by a human. Zero novel signal, zero coverage of the tail we actually care about. It is the reproduction of a 2011 result, not an advance on it.
Byproduct
Every curated chain it surfaces becomes a labelled positive for evaluating the other two routes. This is the cheapest way we have to grow past the ~100-pair ground truth.

The Rarity Index

Recommended as the product

Ship: a melodic figure goes in; “this figure appears in N works, M of them predating <date>” comes out, with the list, the dates, and a rarity percentile. It never says who copied whom — that is the design, not a limitation.

Settles
Whether rarity discriminates at all. Do known hooks separate from musical archetypes in the frequency distribution, or is everything common? If they don’t separate, the index is still the honest denominator — but the wider ambition is dead, and we’d know in week ten instead of month nine.
Build
Start symbolic and skip transcription entirely for v1 — that removes the single largest unmeasured risk from the critical path. Index Themefinder’s five encodings as n‑grams into ordinary posting lists; a query is a lookup and a date filter. Nothing here needs a GPU or a model.
Estimate
5–7 weeks · one engineer · no GPU · corpus licensing TBD
What kills it
The denominator problem. A count is only evidence if the corpus is representative, and the available symbolic corpora skew folk and classical — not commercial pop, which is where the buyers are. “Appears in 1,412 recordings” is worthless if a court can ask what those 1,412 recordings were drawn from and we can’t answer well.
Can’t do
Take audio in, until the transcription spike says it safely can. v1 accepts notation, MIDI, or a hummed query converted by hand.
Why it’s the bet
It is the only route whose output is admissible evidence rather than the kind of testimony courts are actively excluding — and the only one that gets more valuable the more corpus you feed it.

The Trace

Not an MVP — defer

Ship: the demo we started from. A snippet goes in; date-ordered candidate relatives come out, with the matching bars highlighted and an explicit verdict — including “no single originator.”

Settles
Whether the end-to-end experience is possible at all. High demo value, high investor value, low information value — because we already know from the literature roughly how it will perform.
Build
ByteCover‑class embeddings at 128–256d into FAISS or pgvector over a bounded catalogue (~50k tracks seeded around the chains the Ledger surfaces). Basic Pitch or Omnizart for transcription. BMM‑Det for localization, with edit costs weighted by rhythmic function rather than flat.
Estimate
10–14 weeks · GPU · audio access required
What kills it
Four things, any one of them: BMM‑Det drops to 0.33 on rewritten melodies, which is exactly what a cross-genre interpolation is; query-by-humming is already at 0.707 on 90k songs and a real catalogue is three orders of magnitude larger; transcription error compounding is unmeasured; and we can only evaluate against ~100 real pairs. Plus we have to legally hold the audio.
Dependency
It cannot state its own abstention rule without the Rarity Index. “This figure is commonplace” requires a corpus-wide count. Built alone, the Trace has no way to avoid confidently naming an originator for a musical building block — the Skidmore and Gray v. Hudson failure mode, and the thing it will generate most of.

The sequence, and its exits

The routes are alternatives on paper and a sequence in practice. What makes it a plan rather than a wish-list is that each stage ends at a gate with a pre-committed answer for both outcomes.

GateWeekThe questionIf yesIf no
13 Are there enough dated, directed edges to browse chains of length ≥3? The lineage browser is already a product. Ship it while building the index. Expected. Confirms the undocumented tail is the whole opportunity. Keep the edges as evaluation data and go straight to the index.
24 Does the melodic relationship survive Basic Pitch and Omnizart on 50 known interpolation pairs? Audio‑first stays alive. The Rarity Index can accept audio queries in v2 and the Trace keeps its front end. The audio‑first architecture is dead. The index stays symbolic; the Trace goes embedding‑only or is shelved. Nobody has published this measurement — we would be first.
310 Does the rarity distribution separate known hooks from musical archetypes? Ship the prior‑art index. Direction reduces to a date join, and the Trace becomes worth attempting. The index still ships as the denominator — it is useful either way — but the ancestry ambition is retired, loudly rather than quietly.
05

Before week one

Four questions that cost days to answer and can each redraw the plan above. None of them are engineering.

Does WhoSampled offer an API, licensed feed, or bulk access?

The research could not establish this either way. It is the single highest-leverage unknown we have: 42,447 of its records are the exact asset the only working ancestry paper in the literature ran on. If a feed exists and is affordable, the Ledger stops being a probe and becomes a real graph. Two days of email and a legal read.

Highest leverage

What audio can we legally hold, and at what scale?

The Trace is impossible without a catalogue we can embed and store. This determines whether route three exists at all, and it should be settled before anyone writes retrieval code, not after.

Blocks the Trace

Is there a commercial-pop symbolic corpus with defensible provenance?

The Rarity Index’s entire defensibility is its denominator. Meertens and Essen are excellent and skew folk; a court will ask what the corpus was drawn from. Finding — or assembling — a pop-weighted corpus we can describe honestly is the difference between evidence and a number.

Blocks the product claim

What is SecondHandSongs’ actual schema?

Claims about its basedOn and derivedWorks fields were refuted in verification — meaning unverifiable from the cited source, not disproven, since the schema page was unreachable. If those fields exist, it is a second curated derivation layer for free. Worth one afternoon.

Cheap to check
What we will not ship, at any stage
  • A similarity percentage presented as a conclusion. That is precisely the testimony Yen argues courts should exclude, and the thing experts are being excluded under Rule 702 for offering.
  • An “X copied Y” output derived from the model. Direction comes from dates and curated edges or it is not claimed.
  • A named originator where the earliest instance is folk or public-domain material. That path returns “no single originator” and says why.
  • A precision or recall figure stated without its denominator, when that denominator is roughly a hundred examples.
One caveat carried forward

The Kjarkas → Lambada → On the Floor chain that started this project was never independently verified in either research pass — treat it as widely reported rather than confirmed. It does not weaken the case for any route here. The point of that example was always that the chain is in a database because a human put it there, and the value of what we build is in the tail where nobody did.
