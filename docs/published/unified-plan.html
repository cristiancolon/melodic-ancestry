Unified plan Rev 1 7 September 2026

Hookline

You drop in an MP3. It finds the hooks, and for each one tells you how ordinary it is and what came before it. Never who copied whom — that is the design, not a limitation.

Why the two tracks were always one

The research track’s destructive half says litigation-derived benchmarks measure whether a pair was sued. Its constructive half already names the replacement: threshold-free metrics with CIs, and rarity / prior-art counts, which are symmetric and not outcome-dependent. That sentence is a product specification — and the product track was already building it.

One is the argument, the other the instrument. What stood between them is the single gap nobody has measured: how transcription error compounds into symbolic matching on commercial pop. Adding MP3 ingestion is what forces that measurement, so the riskiest dependency and the research contribution turn out to be the same object.

The merge is not a compromise

The two documents looked like separate strategies because one was a critique and the other was a build. They are the same argument seen from both ends.

The pivot How transcription error compounds into downstream symbolic matching on commercial pop is unmeasured anywhere in the literature. The old plan read that as a reason to stay symbolic. Inverted: it is the one place a small team can produce a first result, it sits directly on the product’s critical path, and the measurement is the product’s confidence model. You cannot ship honest intervals on N and M without it, and nobody has it.

Three things audio unlocks that symbolic-only cannot reach

The denominator

A pop corpus, rebuilt

The index’s whole defensibility is what it counts against, and the available symbolic corpora skew folk and classical. Müllensiefen & Pendzich got 90% (18/20) on MCIR cases weighting pitch-interval profiles for rarity against 14,063 pop songs — the strongest published evidence that rarity discriminates, measured on pop. BMM-Det’s authors record that the dataset is not public. The precedent exists and the corpus does not.

Metric stress

What the benchmark destroyed

MCIC’s MIDI has rests removed in 97.34% of transitions, so cumulative onset no longer equals score position and Savage’s stressed / unstressed levels are unrecoverable from it. Beat and downbeat tracking on audio recovers them directly. The rhythm-weighted edit distance both prior documents call for is only implementable on the audio path.

Hook detection

No query to write

The interesting figure in a song is the repeated one, and self-similarity over audio finds it without anyone specifying a query. This is the difference between something you enjoy using and a tool you must already know how to drive — and it costs one more stage in a pipeline that is already running.

02

Input and output

One MP3 in. A provenance card per hook out — a count with its list, never a verdict about people.

Input

  • An MP3, or anything decodable — a clip, a full track, a phone recording of someone humming.
  • Optionally a reference date. Defaults to the recording’s own release date via the Ledger, or the user supplies it.
  • Symbolic input stays first-class. MIDI and notation are the high-confidence stratum, and the fallback when the audio path abstains.

What it will never output

  • A similarity percentage presented as a conclusion.
  • An “X copied Y” claim derived from the model. Direction comes from dates and curated edges or it is not claimed.
  • A named originator where the earliest instance is folk or public-domain material.
  • A count without its denominator, or an N without its interval.

Enforced in the output schema, not in prose. The abstention paths are not error handling — they are four of the seven possible outputs.

03

How it works

Four bands. The audio front end produces a figure and an uncertainty; the index turns that into a count with an interval; the gate decides whether a count is worth reporting at all.

Error-aware retrieval — the technical bet

At 0.35 note-level F, a single top-1 transcription is not a usable query. The hypothesis under test is that the correct notes are present in the top-k with recoverable posterior mass even when they are not ranked first — so the index is queried with a lattice, and what comes back is a distribution over N and M rather than a point estimate off one wrong reading. Prior-art counts have never carried uncertainty, because they have always been compiled by hand.

Evaluation — known-item retrieval, not accuracy

This is where the critique becomes the engineering protocol. We do not evaluate against litigation labels. The metric is: given the derivative’s MP3, does the known ancestor appear in the returned prior art, and at what rank? Recall@k over the Ledger’s curated chains — symmetric, threshold-free, no outcome labels, and a test set that grows as the Ledger grows instead of being capped at roughly a hundred pairs.

04

What is actually new

Stated as things that are true of no existing system, so they can be checked and killed.

  • Error-aware retrieval

    Transcription uncertainty propagated as a lattice into n-gram counting, so the output is a distribution over N and M rather than a point estimate off a single wrong transcription. No published prior-art count carries uncertainty, because none has been produced by machine.

  • The compounding curve

    How much of a known melodic relationship survives audio → symbolic on commercial pop, as a function of n-gram length, encoding, arrangement density, and vocal versus instrumental lead. Unmeasured anywhere in the literature — and it sits at the front of any audio-first pipeline, so everyone downstream of it is guessing.

  • Computational prior art

    In Structured Asset Sales v. Sheeran the court counted twenty-nine prior uses of the progression and twenty-three more predating the defendant’s release, and that enumeration carried the holding — compiled by hand. A machine-produced enumeration with a stated denominator and a stated error model is the same evidentiary form, untested, and on the admissible side of the line Yen draws.

06

Before week one

Six questions that cost days to answer and can each redraw everything above. None of them are engineering.

01

Does WhoSampled offer an API, licensed feed, or bulk access?

Highest leverage. 42,447 directed, dated records are the exact asset the only working ancestry paper ran on. If a feed exists and is affordable, the Ledger stops being a probe and becomes a real graph.

02

What audio can we legally hold, and at what scale?

Note the asymmetry the old plan missed: transient per-query decoding for the front end is a far smaller ask than a stored corpus. Separate the two before asking anyone.

03

Is there a commercial-pop symbolic corpus with defensible provenance?

If one exists, S2 may not need building at all. If none does, building it is the project.

04

What is SecondHandSongs’ actual schema?

Claims about its basedOn and derivedWorks fields were refuted in verification — unverifiable from the cited source, not disproven, since the page was unreachable. If they exist, it is a second curated derivation layer for free. One afternoon.

05

Licence audit of the audio stack

Demucs, Basic Pitch, CREPE, madmom, Essentia. Several are AGPL, non-commercial, or carry patent notes. Do not assume — check each, and separate “may we run it locally” from “may we ship it.”

06

Where do 50 known interpolation pairs with obtainable audio come from?

E1 is not executable without them. Ledger chains, WhoSampled, and the Cronin/MCIR lineage are the candidates.
